## 0. What the campaign settles, in one paragraph

The five-year projection that closed the trajectory instrument now has a mechanical account in
which every link's ingredients are measured; the causal chaining from column corruption to Newton
failure remains inference, as §1 states, because the controlled experiment is a forbidden repair.
SciPy `num_jac`'s per-component step factor ratchets ~one decade per Jacobian refresh (16 of the
base leg's 17 post-init refreshes; one was flat) with no ceiling and no reset — **in every measured
configuration that refreshes its Jacobian in the collision-dominated epoch, including the healthy
one**; only the N=7 segment, where no refresh ever fires the ratchet, is exempt. The ratcheted
columns of the 60/30 Jacobian are measurably corrupted at the creep states themselves (median
~1–1.6% relative, worst column 234× wrong; among the 51 recomputed ratcheted columns the 17 most
deviant are all ratcheted — a subset relation guaranteed by the measurement design, since only
ratcheted columns were recomputed). The true operator at those same creep
states is Hurwitz (abscissa ≈ −5.75, zero unstable modes), so the corruption — not the physics —
feeds the Newton-failure loop that pins the BDF order at 1. The completing 48/24 configuration
carries the *identical* ratchet to 1e18×√EPS and completes anyway: the ratchet is universal, and
what distinguishes stalling from completing is only whether the corrupted columns break Newton.
The pathology is epoch-graded — severe at N ≤ 1, ambiguous at N = 2, gone by N = 5 — fading
monotonically as the collision rates die. And the reason no published code ever reported this:
LSODA never calls `num_jac` — ODEPACK recomputes its finite-difference increment fresh at every
Jacobian evaluation and carries no persistent per-component factor (U10; that FortEPiaNO, NEVO and
Bennett et al. are LSODA-family is the earlier evidence-acquisition campaign's finding, cited
across campaigns rather than remeasured here). The runaway is specific to SciPy's Python
`BDF`/`Radau` path.
